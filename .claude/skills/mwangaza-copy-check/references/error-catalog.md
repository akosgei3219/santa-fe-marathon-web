# What wrong Mwangaza copy has actually looked like

Every entry here really happened while rebuilding the Mwangaza site in August and
September 2026. New mistakes tend to look like old ones.

## 1. The club mixed up with the race
A third-party homepage brief (15 Sep) named **Santa Fe YouthWorks** and **"local run
shops"** as the club's partners and asked for a **"charity partners"** statistic.
YouthWorks cooks the half marathon's post-race brunch; the charities are the race's;
run shops were invented. Briefs also reached for race numbers like **7,000 ft**.
**Lesson:** for every organisation, number or date, ask "is this the club, or the race?"

## 2. Invented statistics and social proof
The same brief wanted **"Youth athletes supported"**, **"Weekly group runs"** counts
and **"100% volunteer led"**. Nobody had supplied any of them. The club has no
testimonials yet.
**Lesson:** a round, confident number with no source is a guess. Placeholder it.

## 3. Selling on the résumé
The brief's mission line ended **"...and athletic excellence"**, and an August intake
asked Kosgei for **personal bests** (corrected 15 Sep). The brand sells the movement,
not results.
**Lesson:** times, wins and "elite" credentials come out, for Kosgei and for the former
elite athletes on the Kenya trips.

## 4. Borrowed prestige
Earlier drafts benchmarked Mwangaza against **Gaynor Train** and an Olympic Trials
comparison; Kosgei had it removed. A 2008 RunnerSpace article about the AmeriKenyan
Running Club's clinics for El Dorado Elementary was offered as background; Kosgei
said it is **background only**, not Mwangaza's story.
**Lesson:** don't make the club look bigger by attaching other organisations' names or
history.

## 5. Elevation drift
The live /corporate-wellness/ page says **7,000 ft**; the club trains at **7,200 ft**
(Kosgei, 15 Sep). The race is 7,000 ft and stays that way.
**Lesson:** never harmonise the two numbers in either direction.

## 6. Audience leaks
- The homepage put "Train in Kenya" right beside the youth program, which read as if
  children could go. The trips are **adults only**; copy now says so.
- The stat **"8–18 · The ages we coach"** became wrong the moment adults were added.
  It now reads "Ages in the youth project".
- "All ages" came in for group runs. It doesn't apply to the youth bands or the trips.
**Lesson:** ask "true for whom?" A fact can be right and still mislead part of the
audience.

## 7. Booking language before booking exists
Kosgei's own words were **"book your trip with us"**, but there are no dates, price or
booking method yet. The button stays "Ask about the next trip".
**Lesson:** a call to action is a promise. It can't be more final than the facts
behind it.

## 8. Misread offers
"Online" could have meant live classes or a membership; Kosgei meant **one-to-one
remote coaching**. "Bike ride" meant **club group rides** as cross-training. "Running
full body works" meant **full-body workout sessions**. Each was asked about before
writing.
**Lesson:** when a new offer arrives as a fragment, don't fill in the details. What a
session or ride contains stays a placeholder until Kosgei describes it.

## 9. Photos that aren't cleared
A photo of Kenyan schoolchildren racing carried a **Jay Waltmunson Photography**
watermark. A race-start photo under a **"CHASING 3"** banner showed identifiable elite
runners at another organisation's event.
**Lesson:** captions and credits are claims. Only the 15 Sep photos credited to Coach
Kosgei are cleared.

## 10. Arrows on the live site
Three live pages end with **"Contact us →"**. Kosgei banned emoji and arrows on the
Mwangaza site on 15 Sep.
**Lesson:** strip them from anything that reuses live-site wording.
